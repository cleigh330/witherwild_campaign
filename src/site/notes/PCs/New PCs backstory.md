A letter was delivered to the leaders in Allula. Rumors of this letter spread through the village, and JEN heard her fair share of them. It was a warning of these likely monstrous outcomes. The letter was signed by someone named Cirelle, and her warning came true. After the message, it did not long before the outbreak of the Witherwild, an endless — and often dangerous — growth of nature and beasts that occurred after Haven stole Nikta’s Reaping Eye. The forest of the treetop village grew exponentially. Vines seemed to have minds of their own and would tangle and choke those who let their guard down. Animals became more ferocious, and, thus, hunting was all the more deadly. Traversing the forest was near impossible for those not skilled with combat or stealth. 

Toward the end of the first week of this phenomenon, Cirelle arrived in Allula. She stood in the middle of the clearing and called out to the people in the treetop village above. JEN gathered with the other villagers and watched with careful curiosity as this elf in rogue-like attire, lightly tan skin, messy waves of auburn hair, and arresting green eyes stared up at them and spoke.

“My name is Cirelle. I am the one who sent the letter. I ask for an audience with you leader.”

Wary of this elf who predicted such a catastrophic imbalance in nature, Omiko, the elder Simiah leader, bellowed, “What you have to say you may speak it freely in this space so all may judge your words.”

The newcomer’s lips set in a hard line before she exhaled. “Very well. I know you must be wary of me, sending a letter as though premonition, but it was not sorcery or even a lucky guess. I am a Wickling that sought out Haven for the hope of a better life, and that probably gives you more caution — a defector returned. But, I was young and foolish. A better life was an illusion. Haven led me into manipulative hands, under the authority of Kreil Dirn, the spymaster, which, in turn, gave me some time with Archmage Phylax. When I found out about Phylax’s plot to steal Nikta’s Repaing Eye, however, I tried everything I could to stop it. It was a shortsighted solution to the Serpent’s Sickness. There was no care for the consequences, only concern for *their* people. They wouldn’t list to a lowly Druid like myself. The letters were the best I could do, and for some…like my former grove…it came too late. 

I’ve made sure they lost my tracks, which wasn’t difficult considering Phylax an Emperor Niclis didn’t think me much a threat on my own, and came to Allula to start the path toward restoring the balance.

All this to give you a proposition. I’ve heard of an ancient magic that is said to hold the power to keep the Witherwild at bay. I want to provide this safety to the people of Allula, but this means I must travel to Seagloam, a town in Haven territory, which isn’t exactly ideal for me. I humbly ask for a small group of fighters capable of traversing the Witherwild with me to retrieve an object that will grant a reprieve in this wilderness.”

As Cirelle finished, JEN could hear the murmurs of the crowd around her. How did she even learn of this magic? What was the object? Could they trust her? Why would she help them? JEN had some of those same questions floating through her mind, but the way this figure commanded the circle and charismatically delivered her plea made JEN all the more intrigued.

Omiko called Cirelle up, and the two disappeared into the leader’s office to discuss this proposition further. Most of the crowd dispersed after a few minutes, but JEN stayed and waited outside the building, busying her time by cleaning and maintaining her weapon and armor. When Cirelle and Omiko exited, Cirelle graciously thanked him and walked away toward the nearest inn. As soon as she left, Omiko signaled JEN over.

“I’ve agreed to send a handful of troops with her. You will accompany her. Keep a watchful eye,” he instructed.

JEN was surprised to hear this news. Omiko was always hospitable but never foolish, and this choice could prove to be the latter. She vowed to stay sharp and act as his better judgement.


***


They headed out the next day. The group comprised of Cirelle, JEN, two simiah rangers, a clank Druid, and faerie sorcerer. It was a group that could silently and carefully traverse the now dangerous landscape. Even so, the trip took a week, requiring more stealth the closer they came to Haven territory. 

One night, part way through their trek, JEN joined Cirelle by the campfire. They stayed quiet for some time before JEN finally broke the silence with a question. She asked Cirelle a question that gnawed at her since the moment Cirelle showed up in Allula: Why are you even trying to help us?

At first, Cirelle gave a wistful smile in thought before answering, “You know, I miss my family, my friends…my grove. By the time I got word to them, after so many years gone, most of them didn’t believe me. After the Haven army tore through the land, only to be followed by the Witherwild, it was too late for them. Instead of caring about the people who mattered most, I put my faith in someone who didn’t care about me at all. I put my trust in those who didn’t care about *my* people, only *their* people. Perhaps, worst of all is that I gave my all to someone who couldn’t even conceive me as a threat.”

A quiet fell over the two of them again. Suddenly, the crackle of the fire sounded deafening as JEN took in that last line. Did Omiko see this elf as a possible enemy if crossed? Should they fear her for some reason?

Cirelle exhaled deeply, “I don’t want any more people to suffer because of my inaction. Consider this work for your people as the first step in my path to redemption.” She chuckled wryly, “Or maybe it’s vengeance.” She nodded to the ornate bracers that JEN still wore despite having on her sleeping attire. “Perhaps you and I aren’t that dissimilar in that way. So, which is it, redemption or vengeance?”

It had been a very, very long time since anyone asked her about her oath. These bracers couldn’t be removed. Not yet. The direct question caught JEN off guard, and it must’ve shown on her face.

“It’s alright,” Cirelle said before JEN could even attempt an answer. “You don’t have to feel any pressure to tell me right now. Just know, whichever the answer, I’m here for you. Whatever you need.” The Druid clapped a hand on JEN’s shoulder with a weight of sincerity before walking off to go to bed.

That night and the days throughout the journey to Seagloam JEN grappled with the promise she made to keep a watchful eye on Cirelle and her desire to simply follow and befriend the woman who bantered and protected the group like she’d always been one of their own.


***


When they finally approached Seagloam, the simiah in the group stopped in awe, staring with wide eyes at the space around them. They asked if anyone was seeing “that.” When the rest of the group asked what they were on about, the simiah told them they were being surrounded by a mystical, iridescent light. Cirelle speculated that it must be part of the magic that kept the Witherwild out of the port town.

Not far from the city, the group was suddenly stopped by a ranger keeping a perimeter of the town. He was a ribbet, like the majority of the population in Seagloam, and he asked what their purpose was for visiting. Cirelle came forward, introduced herself and her group, and told him that she was seeking Pasquale to find information about old magic. With caution, the ribbet looked over the group with studious eyes before he introduced himself as LOGAN and instructed them to follow him into the city. They were to stay close to him as they moved through the town.

Seagloam was a charming port city with cobbled streets and old town flare. The ribbets were all out and busy about their normal lives as though the Witherwild didn’t even exist. The group was shocked to see so many living without being affected by it. LOGAN asked them questions about the Witherwild as they traveled to the Blooming Lily. Curiously, JEN noticed that Cirelle pulled her hood well over her hair and obscured her face as they walked through the streets.

Upon arriving, LOGAN called out to the shopkeep, Pasquale. The ribbet shuffled from the back of his store amongst the books, herbs, potions, and knock knacks. His eyes scanned the group before him until they settled on Cirelle.

He sighed, “Cirelle. I wish I could say it’s a pleasure to see you, but—“

She raised a gentle hand and interrupted him, “I know. I’m not here on behalf of Kreil. In fact, I’m here against him. Come on, Pasquale, you know the real me. It took me a long time to get back to her, but I’m ready to return to my old self. I’m looking to make things right.” She leaned on the counter between them. “The balance of our world is off kilter and needs to be restored. I need your help..”

It was clear that he was conflicted with her words. “You think it’s that simple?”

Her eyes narrowed a touch. “You haven’t seen it out there. You’re all sheltered here.”

“Not what I meant. Haven tore into your bones and kicked that girl from the grove out. Your own Serpent’s Sickness runs deep.”

Cirelle’s jaw tensed. “That’s not true. I tried desperately to get to the grove — any of the groves out there — but there wasn’t enough time. This isn’t about me. While you may not see it, the danger out there is brutal and real.”

“I don’t see it? You think when our trade slowed and scouts didn’t return that we felt nothing?” He argued.

“No, I’m sure you did, but that is a constant for Fanewick. The Wicklings endure every day,” she asserted.

He scoffed, “You led a cushy life in Haven. Don’t act like you share their hardship.”

”That’s not how I’d explain the recent years under the Spymaster, and you have no idea how long I’ve been butting heads with him before I finally went my own way.” She shook her head, “I’m not here to argue about semantics or a timeline. I’m sorry for shit that happened when I worked for that arsehole. I am trying to do everything I can to fix what’s wrong out there. Will you help me?”

”What are you expecting from me?”

“The magic. This place is untouched. They,” she pointed to the simiah, “saw some…glow as we approached. Is that part of it? I need something that will stop the growth so Alula can stabilize.“ When he started to laugh, she persisted, “This isn’t a joke. I came to you, Pasquale, knowing this would be a stretch, but I had to try. I’ve got too many wrongs to right, I know, including my own to you, but I came all this way hoping you’d remember who I was from the grove. You’ve got answers that’ll help me. I know it. Are you really going to have had me go through all the trouble of getting here just to turn me away?” She gave him a grin and shoved a hand into her bag. “Surely, this will help to start.” Cirelle tossed a coin purse onto the counter. “I’ve got something else I’ll be able to barter if you’re willing, but it’ll come at a later date.”

Pasquale stared at the bag for a moment before grabbing it and feeling the weight in his hand. “Come with me. We’ll talk in the back. Your party can wait here. LOGAN, keep an eye on them for me?”

Before turning away, Cirelle looked over those with her and gave a firm nod to JEN, acknowledging she would be back. LOGAN nodded as Cirelle walked into the back room with Pasquale. The two were visible still to the group, but they could no longer hear them.

JEN sat down at a small table in the corner of the room by some bookshelves. LOGAN sat opposite her and asked if she felt Cirelle was trustworthy. JEN shrugged and told him that so far she hasn’t had a reason to distrust her. Everything she’s said has seemed sincere. She asked him why he asked her specifically. This time, he shrugged and said that she seemed like someone who would be honest. Something about this ribbet was so earnest that a tiny small marked JEN’S face.

When Cirelle came back out, she gathered the group and told them that Pasquale agreed to help, but it would take two days to complete the project. She asked if LOGAN knew somewhere they could stay in the meantime. He nodded and led them to an inn. Cirelle insisted on paying for the group’s stay, and none of them fought against it. They were all more than happy to have the comfort of bed and warm meals and ale.

That first night, as they sat around the hearth drinking, LOGAN joined them. Once most of the group resigned to their rooms, LOGAN and JEN asked Cirelle the question that popped in their minds from the beginning of her encounter with Pasquale. How does she know him, and what did she do that made him so wary to offer his help in the first place?

She took a long sip of her ale. Her eyes were somewhere distant. “He was once part of my grove. When I was young, he taught a lot of us kids magic before he left on his own journey, which landed him here. Once we reconnected, I was well under the influence of Kreil. It was the extortion mostly that made him turn his back on me. I hated doing it, but Kreil made me do it to prove myself in the guild, particularly as I worked to be Kreil’s right hand. If I could do something cruel to Pasquale, then the Spymaster could see where my loyalties lied.” She sighed, “Make no mistake, I don’t take pride in what I did, especially to Pasquale. Like I said before, a lot of atonements.” Her eyes danced between them, focusing on JEN. “You’ll keep me honest, yeah?”


***


After those few days, LOGAN came to the inn to fetch the group. They headed back to the Blooming Lily. A large wagon was there with a covered package. The four horses attached whinnied and shook the reins. Cirelle peaked under the canvas and shook hands with Pasquale, and he handed her a wrapped bundle. They spoke in hushed voices that were impossible to hear. 

As they prepared for their departure, LOGAN approached Cirelle and offered to be an escort back to Alula. Part of him wanted to make sure the package was safely in Alula being used for the intended purpose. Another part of him wanted to see the Witherwild with his own eyes and satisfy the call to adventure. And, another part still didn’t want to say goodbye to his newly acquainted friends. Cirelle placed a hand on his shoulder and welcomed him along.


***


Due to having to traverse the overgrowth of the Witherwild and its new predators, the trip back with a large horse drawn wagon took longer than their journey to Seagloam. Their luck turned for the worst because on the last two days of their journey, the back wheels on the wagon broke. In order to finish the delivery, Cirelle turned herself into a massive elephant, and they used rope and the tarp to create a way for her to haul what the group learned was a large lamplight. She refused to let anyone else carry a literal heavy burden. The horses were split amongst the group to ride for the remainder of their return.

Once back in Alula, Cirelle worked with a few other druids to use vines to hoist and hold the Lamplight in the center of the clearing. She poured in a silver dust from the satchel Pasquale gave her followed by a light pink liquid. The Lamplight glowed, and those ancestries closely tied to nature saw the iridescent glow seep through the space. The people talked of the magic and the value of keeping someone like Cirelle around. Standing in the wonder amongst the other citizens of Alula, JEN offered LOGAN a place to stay, and should he wish to make roots in this treetop village, she extended a helping hand to do so.

Over the next few days, the Witherwild in Alula was tamed. After a week, Alula felt nearly back to its old self in many ways. Cirelle stayed and made herself a known and friendly face in the settlement. She and LOGAN actually spent many days out scouting and gathering food and resources for those in Alula. Within a month, Omiko fell ill. Not long after, he passed. As the entire village gathered for his funeral, whispers of who his successor would be began to stir. That night, in a village-wide gathering, a majority called for Cirelle to step up.

She took center stage in the clearing and spoke to the people, “I do not wish my name to be associated in a position of power so openly. My work is best done in the shadows where no one from Haven knows I still work to dismantle their actions.”

Someone in the crowd stated, “Like a wraith.”

Lost from Cirelle’s line of sight, JEN, upset from Omiko’s passing and annoyed this transfer of power was being done so hurriedly, mumbled begrudgingly louder than she meant, “Yeah, the Fanewraith.”

A faerie next to her caught the title, and she yelled out, “The Fanewraith!”

Soon, it became like a chant. Cirelle stood amongst them in awe. As JEN and LOGAN looked on from above, they could see that familiar look on Cirelle’s face. The gears were turning. She bowed, accepting the title. Bitterly, JEN turned away and left.

That night, LOGAN visited her and mentioned that he noticed her displeasure with Cirelle’s succession. JEN mentioned Omiko entrusting her to keep an eye on Cirelle, and it being very convenient for Cirelle to gain such a standing so quickly. What bothered her more is how easy it was to like and trust the elf. Perhaps it was all just an unfortunate coincidence. The two agreed to stay close in Cirelle’s circle and participate in her plans against Haven. And, over the next year, fortunately or frustratingly, Cirelle did nothing that made them distrust her, and Cirelle relied heavily on them; the three became tight-knit.

However, there was always the question that tugged in the back on JEN’s mind if Cirelle could be trusted in the long run, though she ceased to voice that to anyone as of late.
